\begin{abstract}
Ocean source separators normally infer a changing background only from the
mixture being separated. We study whether a causal forecast of the expected
background, computed from prior soundscape measurements, improves separation.
The proposed system encodes historical one-third-octave levels, predicts the
current background spectrum, and injects it into BS-RoFormer band tokens by
concatenation, feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM), or cross-attention.
We use time-disjoint deployment splits, paired SI-SDR evaluation, and a
real-time factor check. A six-family surrogate study selects FiLM: it improves
SI-SDR improvement (SI-SDRi) over an unconditioned separator by $0.34$ dB on
768 paired examples, while JS divergence alone does not predict gain. We then
use 42 hours of continuous real hydrophone audio and compare 5-s, 30-s, 1-min,
and 10-min prediction windows with a fixed one-hour history. A validation rule
retains persistence unless the learned forecast beats it. All resolutions yield
significant paired separation gains on 1,024 held-out real-background clips;
5 s is best at $+1.226$ dB (95\% interval $[1.082,1.381]$), with real-time
factor 0.0024. Only the 5-s learned forecast beats persistence on validation,
showing that temporal conditioning helps even when elaborate dynamics do not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Passive acoustic backgrounds vary with shipping, weather, biology, and season.
A separator that only observes the current mixture must estimate both the
target and that background at once. Long-context soundscape forecasting offers
an additional, strictly causal signal: what the background was expected to look
like immediately before the mixture arrived. Our hypothesis is that this prior
helps a separator reject predictable energy while retaining an unexpected
injected source.

We combine the band-split RoPE Transformer (BS-RoFormer) architecture
\cite{lu2023bsroformer} with a causal recurrent forecast encoder. The
contributions are: (i) a differentiable forecast--separation model; (ii) matched
ablations of concatenation, FiLM, and cross-attention against no forecast;
(iii) a leakage-audited deployment split; (iv) an evaluation protocol that
 reports separation, forecast error, uncertainty, and computational usability;
(v) a six-family source generator with paired analysis by spectral shift,
source--background overlap, and source entropy; and (vi) a continuous real-audio
study at four sub-hour prediction resolutions.

\section{Data and waveform construction}
We use NOAA--Navy SanctSound hourly one-third-octave level (TOL) products
\cite{noaa2020sanctsound}. MB03 deployments 01 and 02 form the training set,
03 is validation, and 04 is held-out test. A sample at hour $t$ is eligible only
when all $H$ preceding hours and hour $t$ are finite and exactly contiguous.
Normalization statistics are fitted on the two training deployments only.

The public TOL product is an aggregate and cannot determine waveform phase.
For each current spectrum $S_t$, we interpolate its dB envelope on FFT bins,
draw a seeded random phase and Rayleigh-like magnitude jitter, invert the FFT,
and normalize RMS. This gives background surrogate $n_t$. It preserves the
coarse measured spectral envelope but not transients, phase, or within-hour
events. It must not be described as recovered raw audio.

For the field experiment we use the corresponding continuous 8-kHz SanctSound
hydrophone recordings directly: 6 hours each from MB03\_01, 02, and 03, and a
continuous 24-hour MB03\_04 test day (42 hours total). One-second Hann
periodograms are integrated into 23 one-third-octave bands from 25 to 4000 Hz,
averaged to 5-s features, and power-aggregated to 30 s, 1 min, or 10 min. No
waveform phase is synthesized in this experiment.

The original injected signal $s_t$ follows the controllable synthesis design
developed in MINUS: harmonic partials plus band-limited noise, attack/release
edges, and low-frequency amplitude modulation. The broad profile adds chirps,
inharmonic tones, impulse trains, frequency-modulated tones, and band-limited
noise, while expanding carrier, modulation, envelope, transient, and noise-mix
ranges. Parameters, family, and source-to-background SNR are drawn
deterministically for every sample. The mixture is $x_t=n_t+s_t$, and clipping
is prevented by common rescaling.

\section{Method}
Let $S_{t-H:t-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times F}$ be normalized historical TOL.
A GRU produces
\begin{align}
 z_t &= \operatorname{LN}(\operatorname{GRU}(S_{t-H:t-1})),\\
 \widehat S_t &= g_\theta(z_t).
\end{align}
Only observations ending at $t-1$ enter the encoder. All three injection
ablations use $c_t=\widehat S_t$; the baseline omits the forecaster.

BS-RoFormer converts mixture STFT bins into band tokens
$X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T\times K\times D}$. Let $P_k(c_t)$ be a learned
band-specific projection and $E(c_t)$ a sequence of embedded forecast bins.
We compare three residual injection operators at the same point before the
time/frequency Transformer:
\begin{align}
\text{Concat: }\widetilde X_k &= X_k+f([X_k;P_k(c_t)]),\\
\text{FiLM: }\widetilde X_k &= (1+\tanh\gamma_k(c_t))\odot X_k+\beta_k(c_t),\\
\text{Cross-attn: }\widetilde X &= X+\operatorname{Attn}(X,E(c_t),E(c_t)).
\end{align}
The residual branches are initialized to zero, so every run begins from the
same audio mapping. Concatenation doubles the local feature width before a
fusion MLP; FiLM directly changes band-token gain and offset; cross-attention
learns content-dependent alignment to forecast-frequency tokens.

The end-to-end objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{wave}}
 +\tfrac12\sum_r\mathcal L_{\mathrm{complex\ STFT},r}
 +\lambda\lVert\widehat S_t-S_t\rVert_1,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=0.2$ for conditioned variants. Gradients from both objectives
reach the forecast encoder; this is joint training, not frozen feature export.

\section{Experimental protocol}
All variants use identical samples, synthesis seeds, optimizer schedule, model
width, matched separator-core initialization, and checkpoint rule. The checkpoint with maximum validation SI-SDR
improvement (SI-SDRi) is evaluated once on MB03\_04. SI-SDR follows the
scale-invariant projection form; improvement subtracts mixture SI-SDR.
We also report residual-background SI-SDR, forecast MAE in dB, inference
real-time factor (RTF), parameter count, and the fraction of paired test examples
where conditioning beats the baseline. Metric uncertainty is a seeded paired
bootstrap over test examples. BSS evaluation background is discussed by
Vincent et al.\ \cite{vincent2006sdr}.

For distribution shift, waveform power is pooled into $K=64$ log-frequency
bins and normalized to $p$. Let $q$ be the mean distribution of 512 draws from
the original narrow harmonic generator. We report the directional
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)$ and use the finite symmetric quantity
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p,q)=\tfrac12D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert m)
 +\tfrac12D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\Vert m),\quad m=(p+q)/2,
\end{equation}
as the primary shift metric \cite{lin1991divergence}. Because distance from the old generator need not
measure source complexity, we also report normalized spectral entropy
$H(p)/\log_2K$ and source--ocean histogram intersection $\sum_k\min(p_k,n_k)$.

\subsection{Conditioning ablation and selection}
Each model uses 4,000 examples per training deployment, 2,000 updates, and SNR
drawn uniformly from $[-12,12]$ dB. We select only by MB03\_03 validation
SI-SDRi: FiLM obtains 5.35 dB, versus 5.17 for concatenation and 4.99 for
cross-attention. On the ordinary 256-example broad MB03\_04 test, their scores
are 5.39, 5.30, and 5.11 dB, respectively; the unconditioned model reaches
5.00 dB. Thus the held-out test confirms, rather than determines, the choice.

At fixed 0 dB, FiLM gains $0.34$ dB over the same unconditioned outputs
($[0.21,0.48]$), concatenation gains $0.15$ dB ($[0.02,0.27]$), and
cross-attention gains $0.01$ dB ($[-0.08,0.10]$). FiLM is a good fit because a
background spectrum naturally specifies which band features to attenuate,
amplify, or shift. It preserves the audio evidence while applying a low-depth
multiplicative/additive prior. Concatenation must relearn a compression of
doubled features, while cross-attention must estimate an alignment from only a
coarse hourly spectrum; its extra flexibility is not rewarded at this data
scale. This explanation is mechanistic and consistent with the results, but is
not a causal proof.

At weak and equal-strength inputs the selected FiLM model improves the mixture
by at least 5.81 dB. At +12 dB, SI-SDRi is near zero because the mixture is already

separate improvement and absolute-quality gates rather than one threshold for
all input strengths.

\subsection{Broad-source distribution study}
To isolate source shape from strength, the main diversity evaluation fixes SNR
at 0 dB and uses 128 held-out MB03\_04
examples from each family. The resulting 768 examples are exactly paired in
ocean waveform, source waveform, timestamp, and SNR.

Across all broad examples the baseline reaches 5.36 dB and FiLM reaches
5.70 dB, a paired gain of $0.34$ dB. The gain--JS correlation is $-0.085$;
the gain falls from $0.54$ dB in the lowest JS quintile to $0.00$ dB in the
highest. Thus ``farther from the original generator'' is not a sufficient
account of when an ocean prior helps.

Spectral entropy is more diagnostic. The gain--entropy correlation is 0.145;
the highest entropy quintile gains $+0.80$ dB ($[0.47,1.17]$). By family,
band-limited noise gains $+0.54$ dB ($[0.19,0.92]$), impulse trains gain
$0.47$ dB ($[0.16,0.79]$), and frequency-modulated targets gain $0.43$ dB
($[0.08,0.82]$). FiLM therefore uses the prior broadly, with its strongest
benefit on diffuse sources; raw JS/KL distance is not the governing variable.

\section{Sub-hour real-waveform study}
The sub-hour experiment fixes history coverage at one hour, giving 720, 120,
60, or 6 causal history steps. For target window $t$, the model receives only
$S_{t-H:t-1}$; the background target is the actual waveform $N_t$. A broad
synthetic source is injected into a clip inside that window,
$x_t=N_t+\alpha s_t$, so clean signal and noise references remain exact.
Absolute clip times and injection seeds are shared across resolutions.

The GRU is first trained for up to 2,500 updates. Because short-window ocean
spectra are highly persistent, its zero-residual initialization is the explicit
step-0 persistence candidate; a learned checkpoint is retained only if it lowers
MB03\_03 forecast MAE. The selected predictor is then frozen while the FiLM
separator trains for at least 3,000 and at most 5,000 updates, with the best
MB03\_03 SI-SDRi checkpoint evaluated once on MB03\_04. Thus prediction quality
cannot be silently degraded to optimize separation.

All four conditioned models pass the five-SNR engineering gate and run at about
RTF 0.0024. The 5-s window has the highest absolute SI-SDRi and paired gain, but
the four gain intervals overlap, so the experiment does not establish a
significant ranking among resolutions. The important negative result is that
more expressive prediction is not automatically useful: only 5 s improves over
persistence on validation (by 1.4\%), while 10-min training has only 60 distinct
next-window examples and strongly overfits. Temporal background information is
useful; with the present data, persistence supplies most of that information.
\section{Usability criteria}
The engineering gate requires overall SI-SDRi of at least 5 dB, at least 3 dB
improvement for input SNR at or below 0 dB, at least 8 dB absolute target
SI-SDR for stronger inputs, and frontend RTF below one. The conditioned model
passes all four checks. Its batched RTF is 0.0040; the complete chunked frontend
processes a 10-second WAV at RTF 0.043 on an RTX 3060. The scientific hypothesis is supported only
if its positive paired SI-SDRi gain is reproduced on unseen, phase-aligned recordings.
If forecast MAE improves while separation does not, the condition is either
irrelevant or poorly injected. If oracle current spectra help but forecasts do
not, forecasting is the bottleneck. These outcomes are explicitly falsifying,
not post-hoc exceptions.

\section{Local application}
The repository includes the validation-selected one-megabyte FiLM checkpoint and a dependency-light local
web application. A user supplies the last 24 hourly TOL rows and a WAV mixture.
The application validates and normalizes history, predicts the current spectrum,
separates overlapping waveform chunks, and exposes target and residual-ocean
audio for playback and download. It also plots the predicted spectrum and
reports duration, latency, and RTF. Requests stay on localhost; no recording or
history is sent to a remote service.

\section{Limitations}
The real-waveform experiment removes the surrogate-background limitation, but
the injected targets remain synthetic and all recordings come from one site.
Only 12 training hours are available; at 10-min resolution this leaves 60
distinct next-window examples, which is inadequate for a flexible GRU. Multiple
recording sites, seasons, training seeds, and real vessel, biological, and sonar
targets are needed before deployment claims can be made.

The six source families are deliberately more diverse than the original
generator, but JS divergence and entropy remain spectral summaries: neither
captures semantic identity, long temporal organization, or recording-device
effects. Family-wise findings should be confirmed with real vessels, animals,
sonar, weather, and anthropogenic events before selecting a production model.

\section{Conclusion}
\method{} supplies a concrete, leakage-resistant route from soundscape
forecasting to source separation and a usable local interface. FiLM is selected
among three matched injection mechanisms. On continuous real backgrounds, all
four sub-hour priors significantly improve separation; 5 s is best in absolute
score with a $+1.226$ dB paired gain. Learned dynamics beat persistence only at
5 s, so current evidence supports temporal conditioning more strongly than a
complex forecaster. The remaining gap is evaluation with real, rather than
synthetically injected, target events across sites and seasons.
