\begin{abstract}
We evaluate FC-BSR with one fixed main setting: a causal GRU, FiLM, a
BS-RoFormer separator, and 5-s windows.  Each supervised example is
$(H_t,N_t,s_t,x_t)$: twelve immediately preceding 5-s background features whose annotated whale-call intervals are
masked, an annotation-clean real 5-s ocean segment, a synthetic marine event,
and their exact mixture.  The available recordings provide 146.4 h of
call-free background, with 37.3 h eligible for training.  The 12-step GRU
reduces held-out forecast MAE from 2.111 to 1.926 dB (8.8\%) against
persistence.  A six-way, matched-condition audit on 4,096 held-out mixtures
finds 9.631 dB target SI-SDRi for both the true BS-RoFormer and Zero-FiLM,
9.497 dB for GRU-Forecast FiLM, and 9.535 dB for Oracle-FiLM using the actual
current 30-band $E(N_t)$.  Oracle minus BS-RoFormer is $-0.096$ dB (day-block 95\% CI
$[-0.238,0.034]$): this 30-band condition has no demonstrated average benefit.
A separate full-waveform oracle copy check learns $x_t-N_t$ on the same held-out
mixtures, showing that the compressed condition is not a full-information oracle.
With the copy interface fixed, an information ladder retains a positive
day-block gain at 128 equal-width frequency groups but not at 64; 30 geometric
bands lose a confirmed gain even at the finest tested time grid.
A separate 13-family study compares other conditioning modes as ablations.  On untouched real calls,
where no isolated stem exists, a frozen event classifier finds no clear
semantic advantage over a matched zero-condition FiLM control.  We therefore
support fully supervised separation on annotated-clean backgrounds, but the
present 30-band forecast/FiLM interface has no established separation gain.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
ocean acoustics, source separation, soundscape forecasting, BS-RoFormer,

\section{Introduction}
Ocean backgrounds vary with shipping, weather, biology, and season
\cite{miksisolds2013indian}.  A mixture-only separator must infer both an
unexpected event and this changing background from the same observation.  We
instead ask whether the preceding soundscape can supply a causal prior.  Our
main data tuple is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &(H_t,N_t,s_t,x_t),\quad x_t=N_t+s_t,\\
 &H_t\xrightarrow{\mathrm{GRU}}\widehat S_t,\qquad
 x_t\xrightarrow[\widehat S_t]{\mathrm{FiLM+BSR}}(\hat s_t,\hat N_t),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $H_t$ excludes annotated call intervals and $N_t$ is a real current
no-call segment.  Training has exact stems, while the separator never receives
$N_t$ or $s_t$ as input.

Time-domain separators \cite{luo2019convtasnet,luo2020dprnn}, attention models
\cite{subakan2021sepformer,wang2023tfgridnet}, and band-split systems
\cite{luo2023bsrnn,lu2023bsroformer} provide strong mixture-only backbones.
Hybrid Transformers and MixIT broaden the architecture and supervision regimes
\cite{rouard2023htdemucs,wisdom2020mixit}, but do not test a forecast of the
upcoming background.  FiLM, cross-attention, and cross-modal separation
\cite{perez2018film,vaswani2017attention,zhao2018pixels} motivate three
interfaces.  Forecasting baselines range from recurrent to linear and
patch-based models \cite{salinas2020deepar,oreshkin2020nbeats,zeng2023dlinear,nie2023patchtst}.

Our contributions are: (1) FC-BSR,\footnote{Code and reproduction instructions:
\url{https://github.com/D-eval/ocean_noise_pred_splite}.} with a single
fixed main setting (GRU+FiLM+BS-RoFormer, 5 s); (2) a reproducible
annotation-aware construction of 146.4 h of current call-free ocean background
and event-masked 5-s histories; (3) a 13-family broad event generator with
seven whale/vessel families in the main DCLDE experiment;
and (4) a frozen-classifier evaluation on untouched real calls against a
zero-condition FiLM control.  TOL-domain,
conditioning-mode, time-scale, and unannotated-background strength studies are
reported as ablations rather than as the primary evidence.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Forecast and conditional separator}
The main history $H_t\in\mathbb R^{12\times30}$ contains 30-band log-power
features computed on twelve preceding 5-s windows; annotated calls and their
2-s guards are excluded.  Features are normalized using training data only.
A residual GRU predicts
\begin{equation}
 z_t=\operatorname{LN}(\operatorname{GRU}(H_t)),\quad
 \widehat S_t=S_{t-1}+g_\psi(z_t).
\end{equation}
Residual initialization represents persistence exactly; a learned predictor is
retained only if validation MAE improves upon persistence
\cite{gneiting2011pointForecast}.

The main FC-BSR maps the mixture STFT to band tokens $X_k$ and applies FiLM
before the axial Transformer:
\begin{align}
\widetilde X_k&=(1+\tanh\gamma_k)\odot X_k+\beta_k.\label{eq:inject}
\end{align}
The separator predicts an event stem; the ocean stem is the mixture residual.
Training combines waveform $L_1$ and multi-resolution complex-STFT losses.
Concatenation and cross-attention are alternative conditioning ablations, not
the deployed FC-BSR configuration.

The main-domain information audit holds the separator backbone, mixtures,
initialization, and training rule fixed.  A true BS-RoFormer bypasses the
condition injector; Zero-FiLM retains the FiLM branch and the same frozen GRU,
but replaces its output by $c=0$.  Shuffled-FiLM uses
$E(N_j)$ from a deterministic half-cycle offset in the clean-window index of
the same split ($j\ne t$); on the held-out test, this selects a different day.
Persistence-FiLM
uses $S_{t-1}$, and Forecast-FiLM uses the frozen GRU output.  Oracle-FiLM
receives $S_t^{\mathrm{oracle}}=E(N_t)$ computed from the actual current clean
background after any shared mixture scaling.  We call this the \emph{30-band
Oracle-FiLM}: $E$ removes temporal structure and phase, so this row bounds
neither a full-background condition nor the value of perfect forecasting with
a richer representation.  It is available only in the controlled experiment.

\subsection{Marine-aware source distribution}
The broad generator contains 13 families: harmonic, linear chirp, inharmonic,
impulse train, frequency-modulated tone, band noise, whale upsweep, downsweep,
arch, S-curve, pulse packet, vessel tonal, and vessel cavitation.  Whale proxies
use one to three localized events with variable duration, curvature, partials,
taper, and flutter.  Vessel-tonal and narrow-band cavitation components draw a
5--20 Hz low-frequency-oscillator amplitude envelope with probability 0.8;
their broadband component is not modulated.  Training SNR is uniform on
$[-18,12]$ dB.  This distribution is intended to cover contour and modulation
patterns, not to claim species identity.

We quantify spectral displacement with directional $D_{\rm KL}(p\|q)$ and
finite symmetric Jensen--Shannon (JS) divergence \cite{lin1991divergence};
spectral entropy and source--ocean overlap are reported separately.  Larger
KL/JS expands coverage but is not treated as semantic diversity.  The DCLDE
main experiment samples only its five whale and two vessel families; all six
main-domain models see the same target distribution.

\subsection{Main data construction and secondary ablations}
The main dataset uses day-disjoint 2-kHz DCLDE 2013 recordings and whale-call
annotations \cite{gillespie2019dclde,shiu2020marine}.  Across 168 h of WAV,
excluding annotated calls and a 2-s guard on each side yields 146.4 h of
annotation-clean 5-s windows.  For each target, the 12 immediately preceding
5-s blocks from the same WAV form a 30-band history; a target is eligible only
when all history blocks are outside annotated calls and their 2-s guards.  This
avoids temporal averaging over an hour and forbids histories from crossing WAV
boundaries.  This leaves 94.5 h eligible overall, split into 37.3/9.7/47.6 h
for train/validation/test.
For each current clean $N_t$, a seven-family whale/vessel synthetic event $s_t$ is mixed to
form $x_t=N_t+s_t$.  The main split is by recording day, and the GRU receives
12 event-masked features from the immediately preceding 5-s windows.

Secondary ablations use NOAA--Navy SanctSound hourly TOL products
\cite{noaa2020sanctsound}.  MB03 deployments 01--02/03/04 are
train/validation/test.  Random-phase inversion creates controlled spectral
surrogates, not recovered audio.  Concatenation, cross-attention, KL/JS
coverage, and the one-hour history-resolution comparison are confined to this
domain.  The separate 42-h SanctSound WAV strength sweep also lacks event
annotations, so it is a useful controlled real-background ablation but is not
counted as clean-background primary evidence.

Complete-reference tiers report SI-SDR and SI-SDRi using scale-invariant
projection \cite{vincent2006sdr,leroux2019sdr}.  All uncertainty intervals are
seeded paired bootstraps, blocked by recording day for the main test.  The event classifier is frozen before any real-event
evaluation.  Current test features are used only as targets for the synthetic
full-reference task, never as GRU input; all histories end before $t$.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\subsection{Conditioning-mode ablation on spectral surrogates}
The primary architecture is fixed to FiLM.  Table~\ref{tab:ablation} compares
it with the alternative interfaces on controlled 5-s spectral-surrogate
examples; on 832 fixed-0-dB pairs (64 per family), FiLM improves 4.850 to
5.541 dB SI-SDRi.  Band-wise scale and bias directly modulate existing band
evidence.  Concatenation must compress doubled features, while cross-attention
must learn alignment from coarse predicted spectra under the same budget.

FiLM gains 0.869, 0.835, 0.919, 0.906, and 0.806 dB on the five whale proxy

\subsection{Primary fully supervised clean-background separation}
The main FC-BSR receives twelve event-masked preceding 5-s features and a current clean
5-s DCLDE segment; the seven-family event is added only after the clean background
is selected.  The model therefore sees $x_t$ and $H_t$, while the exact $N_t$
and $s_t$ are supervision.  The training split contains 37.3 h of
eligible clean current audio.  The 12-step GRU forecasts the next 5-s band
spectrum; validation MAE is 1.926 dB versus 2.111 dB for persistence (8.8\%
lower).
The older two-row comparison labeled a Zero-FiLM checkpoint as
``BS-RoFormer.''  We retain its exact trained waveform mapping as the Zero
control, attach the same frozen GRU in a bypassed branch, and train a true
injector-bypassed BS-RoFormer.  All six rows in Table~\ref{tab:cleanmain} use
the same mixtures, transferred backbone initialization, optimizer budget,
validation selection, and 4,096 test examples from three held-out days.
Figure~\ref{fig:cleanstrength} stratifies these same samples by input SNR.

The architecture effect, Zero minus BS-RoFormer, is $-0.0002$ dB (95\% CI
$[-0.0068,0.0088]$).  Forecast minus BS-RoFormer is $-0.134$ dB
($[-0.327,0.125]$); Oracle minus BS-RoFormer is $-0.096$ dB
($[-0.238,0.034]$).  Oracle minus Forecast is only $+0.039$ dB
($[-0.090,0.155]$).  Shuffling and persistence also fail to show a positive
average over Zero.  Thus the matched controls do not establish that temporal
forecast information improves this separator.  Oracle's higher validation
score (11.372 versus 10.946 dB for BS-RoFormer) does not transfer to a clear
held-out gain.  With only three test days, the intervals leave small effects
unresolved; the 30-band oracle supplies more information, but separately optimized
FiLM models do not form a guaranteed performance ordering.

\paragraph{Full-waveform oracle copy check.}
To test whether losing information in $E$ explains the negative 30-band result,
we freeze the same trained BS-RoFormer and fit a pointwise, three-coefficient
head on 8,192 training mixtures:
\begin{equation}
\hat s_t=w_b\hat s_{\rm BSR}(x_t)+w_xx_t+w_cc_t.
\end{equation}
The head starts at $(1,0,0)$, exactly reproducing BSR, and its waveform-MSE
global optimum is solved by least squares.  We fit the \emph{same} head with
zero, wrong-time, and actual current background waveforms; validation and
testing use 4,096 examples each, with the test IDs, SNRs, mixture scores, and
frozen BSR outputs checked against Table~\ref{tab:cleanmain}.
For $c_t=N_t$, the learned weights are $(0,1,-1)$ to numerical precision:
the head copies the exact answer $x_t-N_t=s_t$.  The maximum test waveform
error is $5.96\times10^{-8}$; the finite SI-SDRi of 80.755 dB is limited by
the metric's numerical floor.  Zero and wrong-time heads receive no useful
background identity and do not improve SI-SDRi (Table~\ref{tab:copy}).  This
is an intentionally noncausal information/path sanity check with a different
interface, not an architecture-matched FiLM ablation or evidence that the
30-band forecast can recover a phase-aligned background waveform.

\paragraph{Oracle information ladder.}
We next reduce the information in the \emph{current} $N_t$ while keeping the
frozen BSR, the three-coefficient head, training examples, and 4,096 matched
test mixtures fixed.  A 512-point Hann STFT at 2 kHz has 257 frequency bins
on a 64-ms hop.  We average background power across either adjacent
equal-width groups (128, 64, 32) or geometric 20--950-Hz groups
(128, 64, 48, 30), and separately pool frames over 0.25, 1, or 5 s.
The retained magnitude is inverted with either the actual $N_t$ phase or the
mixture $x_t$ phase.  Geometric groups absorb edge bins; only 95 of the
nominal 128 geometric groups contain an STFT bin.  The 30-band/5-s rung is
analogous to, but not numerically identical with, the original $E(N_t)$.

Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} identifies where this \emph{copy path} ceases to show
a held-out gain.  Exact amplitude with mixture phase still adds 11.164 dB
over BSR (day-block CI $[10.889,11.594]$).  With equal-width groups,
128 retains $+2.067$ dB ($[0.969,3.386]$), while 64 has $-0.623$ dB
($[-1.404,0.259]$): the confirmed gain disappears between these levels.
For geometric bands, nominal 128 already gives $-0.529$ dB
($[-1.257,0.319]$), and 30 per-frame bands give $-0.672$ dB
($[-1.323,0.086]$).  Retaining the true phase at 30 per-frame bands gives
only $+0.117$ dB ($[-0.671,1.084]$).  By contrast, keeping all 257 bins but
pooling power to 1 s still yields $+0.585$ dB with mixture phase
($[0.285,1.132]$); the 5-s version yields $+0.249$ dB
($[-0.044,0.495]$).  Thus this interface is especially sensitive to
frequency averaging and the grouping rule, while time averaging alone is
less destructive.  These are sufficiency results for the fixed waveform
copy head, not information-theoretic limits or new FiLM results.

The harder fixed-5-s experiment on unannotated SanctSound WAV compares
Zero-condition FiLM, a current-spectrum oracle, causal forecast, and a
time-shuffled condition on 1,152 paired test mixtures.  This oracle belongs to
the secondary data domain; Table~\ref{tab:cleanmain} uses the actual
annotation-clean DCLDE $N_t$.
Averaged across training SNRs, the baseline scores 23.470 dB and Forecast-FC-BSR
22.871 dB ($\Delta=-0.599$, CI $[-0.851,-0.346]$); even the oracle is lower.

Figure~\ref{fig:strength} gives the more informative strength sweep.  Forecast
conditioning helps weak targets by 0.728, 0.643, 0.525, 0.366, and 0.157 dB at

shuffled condition also helps the weakest targets but collapses as SNR rises;
therefore weak-target gain alone cannot prove that temporal forecast accuracy,
rather than regularization or extra capacity, is responsible.

\subsection{Real events without separated ground truth}
Following learned reference-free metrics \cite{kilgour2019fad,grais2019referenceless}
and semantic audio embeddings \cite{wu2023clap,xiao2024clapscore}, we train,
calibrate, and freeze a binary event classifier (test AUROC .9723, AP .9755,
F1 .9275).  Mixture consistency imposes $\hat N=x-\hat s$.  We restore
$\hat s$ at gains $\{-60,-30,-24,-18,-12,-6,0\}$ dB and test whether event
probability recovers monotonically.  Identity and all-to-event controls reject
trivial solutions; changes outside annotated intervals audit preservation.

The same frozen 12-step event-masked forecaster used by the main setting supplies
the causal history features.  Because recent call activity is masked, 103 real
test calls retain a full eligible 60-s history.  Table~\ref{tab:events} shows
the untouched-call results: FC-BSR trends toward lower residual event
probability and off-event change, while event-stem probability is nearly
unchanged.  The day-block intervals include zero for residual probability
$\Delta=-.0731$ ($[-.2090,.0294]$), event-stem probability $\Delta=+.0022$
($[-.0180,.0304]$), and monotonicity $\Delta=+.0178$ ($[-.0333,.0429]$).
Off-event relative error decreases by $.0248$ ($[-.0456,-.0018]$).  This small
semantic set is suggestive but not decisive; separated-stem ground truth is
still unavailable.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The main-domain 30-band Oracle is a diagnostic of the existing interface: even when FiLM receives
$E(N_t)$ from the exact current clean waveform, its held-out mean does not
exceed the mixture-only BS-RoFormer, and its paired interval spans zero.
The full-waveform copy check succeeds exactly, so the current negative result
cannot be interpreted as evidence that the true background lacks useful
information.  It leaves the relative roles of 30-band compression and FiLM
injection unresolved.  The fixed-head ladder shows a clear representation
bottleneck: even mixture-phase conditions retain a confirmed gain at 128
equal-width frequency groups or 1-s full-bin temporal pooling, whereas 64
equal-width groups and 30 geometric bands do not.  The original FiLM result
cannot be assigned to this bottleneck alone because its interface differs.
Zero-FiLM is effectively tied with BS-RoFormer, while Forecast is no better
than either Zero or Shuffled.  The result does not isolate prediction error
as the bottleneck; a five-second, 30-band static summary and its FiLM interface
may be too coarse for the separator's time-frequency tokens.  FiLM remains the
best interface on controlled spectral surrogates, and the secondary unlabeled
WAV sweep shows weak-target gains, but neither establishes a main-domain gain.
No decisive forecast advantage appears on untouched whale calls.  The main
test has three held-out recording days, so small or site-dependent effects
remain possible.

The local frontend is usable for inspection: on a CPU smoke test, one 5-s
chunk ran at RTF 0.014 after history features were prepared; the interface
displays forecasts and permits playback and download of both estimates.  This
does not establish field deployment.
The next representation to test is a time-frequency innovation map comparing
the current mixture with predicted background, rather than only a static band
mean.  Further work should cover more sites and seasons and obtain isolated
or stronger partial references for real events.  Classifier-based restoration
is evidence of semantic transfer, not a replacement for waveform fidelity.
