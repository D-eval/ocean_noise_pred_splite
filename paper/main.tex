\begin{abstract}
We test whether past ocean soundscape can predict a background prior that
improves separation of a future event.  DCLDE examples combine an
annotation-clean real 5-s ocean recording with a synthetic marine event;
the preceding 60-s history masks annotated calls.  An initial 30-band GRU
forecast injected through FiLM does not improve BS-RoFormer on 4,096 held-out
mixtures (9.497 versus 9.631 dB target SI-SDRi), even with the true current
30-band spectrum as an Oracle (9.535 dB).  An information ladder instead
identifies a useful 128-group, time-resolved representation.  We feed its
background map and explicit mixture-minus-background innovation through a
matched residual adapter.  The true-background Oracle reaches 16.767 dB;
fixed-mean and wrong-day map controls reach 12.156 and 11.944 dB, while
past-60-s persistence and a frozen GRU forecast reach 14.295 and 14.351 dB.
Persistence exceeds the fixed-mean and wrong-day controls by 2.138 and
2.351 dB, respectively. Forecast exceeds persistence by
$0.056$ dB (recording-day paired 95\% CI $[0.036,0.074]$), but remains
$2.416$ dB below Oracle. Thus the residual interface itself helps, and
time-relevant historical background supplies a further substantial gain;
this GRU adds little beyond persistence. Synthetic targets and only three
held-out days limit generalization; classifier tests on untouched calls do
not establish real-event superiority. On the same 103 real calls, causal
conditions preserve off-event audio better than wrong-day Shuffled, whereas
Shuffled scores better on the classifier's two event-probability measures;
the real-event ranking is therefore unresolved.
\end{abstract}

ocean acoustics, source separation, soundscape forecasting, BS-RoFormer,

\section{Introduction}
Ocean backgrounds vary with shipping, weather, biology, and season
\cite{miksisolds2013indian}.  A mixture-only separator must infer both an
unexpected event and this changing background from the same observation.  We
instead ask whether the preceding soundscape can supply a causal prior.  Our
supervised data tuple and main model are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &x_t=N_t+s_t,\qquad \widehat B_t=P_\psi(H_t),\\
 &\widehat R_t=\log|X_t|-\widehat B_t,\\
 &[X_t,\widehat B_t,\widehat R_t]
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Residual+BSR}}(\hat s_t,\hat N_t),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $H_t$ excludes annotated call intervals and $N_t$ is a real current
no-call segment.  Training has exact stems, while the separator never receives
$N_t$ or $s_t$ as input.  Here $X_t$ denotes the current mixture STFT and
$\widehat B_t$ its predicted background log-magnitude map.  The 30-band FiLM
precursor failed on held-out audio, motivating an information ladder and the
128-group residual interface in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}.

Time-domain separators \cite{luo2019convtasnet,luo2020dprnn}, attention models
\cite{subakan2021sepformer,wang2023tfgridnet}, and band-split systems
\cite{luo2023bsrnn,lu2023bsroformer} provide strong mixture-only backbones.
Hybrid Transformers and MixIT broaden the architecture and supervision regimes
\cite{rouard2023htdemucs,wisdom2020mixit}, but do not test a forecast of the
upcoming background.  FiLM, cross-attention, and cross-modal separation
\cite{perez2018film,vaswani2017attention,zhao2018pixels} motivate three
interfaces.  Forecasting baselines range from recurrent to linear and
patch-based models \cite{salinas2020deepar,oreshkin2020nbeats,zeng2023dlinear,nie2023patchtst}.

Our contributions are: (1) a 128-group time-resolved causal background map
with an explicit innovation and residual BSR interface;\footnote{Code and
reproduction instructions: \url{https://github.com/D-eval/ocean_noise_pred_splite}.}
(2) an annotation-aware construction of real clean ocean backgrounds and
event-masked 60-s histories; (3) a six-way matched test separating adapter
capacity, wrong-day background, persistence, learned forecast, and current
Oracle information; and (4) a diagnostic chain from failed 30-band FiLM to
the frequency-resolution ladder.  Broad source and real-event studies assess
coverage and transfer; they are secondary to the full-reference DCLDE test.

\section{Method}
\subsection{High-resolution causal residual separator}
The main history $H_t\in\mathbb R^{12\times79\times128}$ contains twelve
strictly prior 5-s, 128-group log-magnitude maps.  A 512-point Hann STFT
with 128-point hop gives 79 frames per window at 2 kHz.  Annotated event
windows are masked and filled only with nearby clean windows within the past
60 s.  A small residual GRU compresses each map into four temporal chunks,
processes the twelve steps, and predicts the next full $79\times128$ map
$\widehat B_t$; its zero-initialized decoder starts at last-map persistence.
The forecaster is trained on clean training days, selected by validation map
MAE, and frozen before separation.

We form $\widehat R_t=\log|X_t|-\widehat B_t$ on the same grid.  The
time-aligned pair $[\widehat B_t,\widehat R_t]$ is projected into
mixture-derived BSR band tokens before axial attention.  The residual
projection starts at zero, so every conditioned separator initially equals
the same transferred mixture-only BSR waveform mapping.  We train with exact
synthetic event stems and a waveform plus multi-resolution STFT loss.
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the data and inference paths.

\subsection{Preliminary 30-band FiLM design}
The precursor history $H_t\in\mathbb R^{12\times30}$ contains 30-band log-power
features computed on twelve preceding 5-s windows; annotated calls and their
2-s guards are excluded.  Features are normalized using training data only.
A residual GRU predicts
\begin{equation}
 z_t=\operatorname{LN}(\operatorname{GRU}(H_t)),\quad
 \widehat S_t=S_{t-1}+g_\psi(z_t).
\end{equation}
Residual initialization represents persistence exactly; a learned predictor is
retained only if validation MAE improves upon persistence
\cite{gneiting2011pointForecast}.

The precursor FC-BSR maps the mixture STFT to band tokens $X_k$ and applies FiLM
before the axial Transformer:
\begin{align}
\widetilde X_k&=(1+\tanh\gamma_k)\odot X_k+\beta_k.\label{eq:inject}
\end{align}
The separator predicts an event stem; the ocean stem is the mixture residual.
Training combines waveform $L_1$ and multi-resolution complex-STFT losses.
Concatenation and cross-attention were also tested as preliminary interfaces.

The preliminary 30-band audit holds the separator backbone, mixtures,
initialization, and training rule fixed.  A true BS-RoFormer bypasses the
condition injector; Zero-FiLM retains the FiLM branch and the same frozen GRU,
but replaces its output by $c=0$.  Shuffled-FiLM uses
$E(N_j)$ from a deterministic half-cycle offset in the clean-window index of
the same split ($j\ne t$); on the held-out test, this selects a different day.
Persistence-FiLM
uses $S_{t-1}$, and Forecast-FiLM uses the frozen GRU output.  Oracle-FiLM
receives $S_t^{\mathrm{oracle}}=E(N_t)$ computed from the actual current clean
background after any shared mixture scaling.  We call this the \emph{30-band
Oracle-FiLM}: $E$ removes temporal structure and phase, so this row bounds
neither a full-background condition nor the value of perfect forecasting with
a richer representation.  It is available only in the controlled experiment.

\subsection{Marine-aware source distribution}
The broad generator contains 13 families: harmonic, linear chirp, inharmonic,
impulse train, frequency-modulated tone, band noise, whale upsweep, downsweep,
arch, S-curve, pulse packet, vessel tonal, and vessel cavitation.  Whale proxies
use one to three localized events with variable duration, curvature, partials,
taper, and flutter.  Vessel-tonal and narrow-band cavitation components draw a
5--20 Hz low-frequency-oscillator amplitude envelope with probability 0.8;
their broadband component is not modulated.  Training SNR is uniform on
$[-18,12]$ dB.  This distribution is intended to cover contour and modulation
patterns, not to claim species identity.

We quantify spectral displacement with directional $D_{\rm KL}(p\|q)$ and
finite symmetric Jensen--Shannon (JS) divergence \cite{lin1991divergence};
spectral entropy and source--ocean overlap are reported separately.  Larger
KL/JS expands coverage but is not treated as semantic diversity.  The DCLDE
main experiment samples only its five whale and two vessel families; all six
main-domain models see the same target distribution.

\subsection{Main data construction and secondary ablations}
The main dataset uses day-disjoint 2-kHz DCLDE 2013 recordings and whale-call
annotations \cite{gillespie2019dclde,shiu2020marine}.  Across 168 h of WAV,
excluding annotated calls and a 2-s guard on each side yields 146.4 h of
annotation-clean 5-s windows.  For each target, the 12 immediately preceding
5-s blocks from the same WAV form a high-resolution history.  Annotated calls
and their 2-s guards are masked; 235 of 68,075 eligible current windows have
one masked historical block, filled by the nearest clean block still within
the preceding 60 s.  Histories never cross WAV boundaries or use the current
window.  This leaves 94.5 h eligible overall, split into 37.3/9.7/47.6 h
for train/validation/test.
For each current clean $N_t$, a seven-family whale/vessel synthetic event $s_t$ is mixed to
form $x_t=N_t+s_t$.  The main split is by recording day, and the GRU receives
only event-masked features from the immediately preceding 5-s windows.

Secondary ablations use NOAA--Navy SanctSound hourly TOL products
\cite{noaa2020sanctsound}.  MB03 deployments 01--02/03/04 are
train/validation/test.  Random-phase inversion creates controlled spectral
surrogates, not recovered audio.  Concatenation, cross-attention, KL/JS
coverage, and the one-hour history-resolution comparison are confined to this
domain.  The separate 42-h SanctSound WAV strength sweep also lacks event
annotations, so it is a useful controlled real-background ablation but is not
counted as clean-background primary evidence.

Complete-reference tiers report SI-SDR and SI-SDRi using scale-invariant
projection \cite{vincent2006sdr,leroux2019sdr}.  All uncertainty intervals are
seeded paired bootstraps, blocked by recording day for the main test.  The event classifier is frozen before any real-event
evaluation.  Current test features are used only as targets for the synthetic
full-reference task, never as GRU input; all histories end before $t$.

\subsection{Preliminary 30-band FiLM audit on DCLDE}
The original FC-BSR receives twelve event-masked preceding 5-s features and a current clean
5-s DCLDE segment; the seven-family event is added only after the clean background
is selected.  The model therefore sees $x_t$ and $H_t$, while the exact $N_t$
and $s_t$ are supervision.  The training split contains 37.3 h of
eligible clean current audio.  The 12-step GRU forecasts the next 5-s band
spectrum; validation MAE is 1.926 dB versus 2.111 dB for persistence (8.8\%
lower).
The older two-row comparison labeled a Zero-FiLM checkpoint as
``BS-RoFormer.''  We retain its exact trained waveform mapping as the Zero
control, attach the same frozen GRU in a bypassed branch, and train a true
injector-bypassed BS-RoFormer.  All six rows in Table~\ref{tab:cleanmain} use
the same mixtures, transferred backbone initialization, optimizer budget,
validation selection, and 4,096 test examples from three held-out days.
Figure~\ref{fig:cleanstrength} stratifies these same samples by input SNR.

The architecture effect, Zero minus BS-RoFormer, is $-0.0002$ dB (95\% CI
$[-0.0068,0.0088]$).  Forecast minus BS-RoFormer is $-0.134$ dB
($[-0.327,0.125]$); Oracle minus BS-RoFormer is $-0.096$ dB
($[-0.238,0.034]$).  Oracle minus Forecast is only $+0.039$ dB
($[-0.090,0.155]$).  Shuffling and persistence also fail to show a positive
average over Zero.  Thus the matched controls do not establish that temporal
forecast information improves this separator.  Oracle's higher validation
score (11.372 versus 10.946 dB for BS-RoFormer) does not transfer to a clear
held-out gain.  With only three test days, the intervals leave small effects
unresolved; the 30-band oracle supplies more information, but separately optimized
FiLM models do not form a guaranteed performance ordering.

\paragraph{Full-waveform oracle copy check.}
To test whether losing information in $E$ explains the negative 30-band result,
we freeze the same trained BS-RoFormer and fit a pointwise, three-coefficient
head on 8,192 training mixtures:
\begin{equation}
\hat s_t=w_b\hat s_{\rm BSR}(x_t)+w_xx_t+w_cc_t.
\end{equation}
The head starts at $(1,0,0)$, exactly reproducing BSR, and its waveform-MSE
global optimum is solved by least squares.  We fit the \emph{same} head with
zero, wrong-time, and actual current background waveforms; validation and
testing use 4,096 examples each, with the test IDs, SNRs, mixture scores, and
frozen BSR outputs checked against Table~\ref{tab:cleanmain}.
For $c_t=N_t$, the learned weights are $(0,1,-1)$ to numerical precision:
the head copies the exact answer $x_t-N_t=s_t$.  The maximum test waveform
error is $5.96\times10^{-8}$; the finite SI-SDRi of 80.755 dB is limited by
the metric's numerical floor.  Zero and wrong-time heads receive no useful
background identity and do not improve SI-SDRi (Table~\ref{tab:copy}).  This
is an intentionally noncausal information/path sanity check with a different
interface, not an architecture-matched FiLM ablation or evidence that the
30-band forecast can recover a phase-aligned background waveform.

\paragraph{Oracle information ladder.}
We next reduce the information in the \emph{current} $N_t$ while keeping the
frozen BSR, the three-coefficient head, training examples, and 4,096 matched
test mixtures fixed.  A 512-point Hann STFT at 2 kHz has 257 frequency bins
on a 64-ms hop.  We average background power across either adjacent
equal-width groups (128, 64, 32) or geometric 20--950-Hz groups
(128, 64, 48, 30), and separately pool frames over 0.25, 1, or 5 s.
The retained magnitude is inverted with either the actual $N_t$ phase or the
mixture $x_t$ phase.  Geometric groups absorb edge bins; only 95 of the
nominal 128 geometric groups contain an STFT bin.  The 30-band/5-s rung is
analogous to, but not numerically identical with, the original $E(N_t)$.

Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} identifies where this \emph{copy path} ceases to show
a held-out gain.  Exact amplitude with mixture phase still adds 11.164 dB
over BSR (day-block CI $[10.889,11.594]$).  With equal-width groups,
128 retains $+2.067$ dB ($[0.969,3.386]$), while 64 has $-0.623$ dB
($[-1.404,0.259]$): the confirmed gain disappears between these levels.
For geometric bands, nominal 128 already gives $-0.529$ dB
($[-1.257,0.319]$), and 30 per-frame bands give $-0.672$ dB
($[-1.323,0.086]$).  Retaining the true phase at 30 per-frame bands gives
only $+0.117$ dB ($[-0.671,1.084]$).  By contrast, keeping all 257 bins but
pooling power to 1 s still yields $+0.585$ dB with mixture phase
($[0.285,1.132]$); the 5-s version yields $+0.249$ dB
($[-0.044,0.495]$).  Thus this interface is especially sensitive to
frequency averaging and the grouping rule, while time averaging alone is
less destructive.  These are sufficiency results for the fixed waveform
copy head, not information-theoretic limits or new FiLM results.

\subsection{Main 128-group residual experiment}
\paragraph{Trained high-resolution Oracle residual separator.}
We next test the representation in an actual BS-RoFormer, rather than only a
frozen linear copy head.  The actual current $N_t$ and mixture $x_t$ are
transformed with a 512-point, 128-hop STFT.  Mean power in 128 equal-width
frequency groups yields time-resolved $\log|B_t|$; the explicit innovation is
$R_t=\log|X_t|-\log|B_t|$ on the same grid.  These maps are interpolated to
the BSR frame grid and projected into its mixture-derived band tokens before
axial attention.  This zero-initialized residualized $[X_t,B_t,R_t]$ input
uses neither FiLM nor a forecaster; its initial waveform output exactly
matches ordinary BSR (maximum difference zero).

Both models use the same day-disjoint DCLDE mixtures, transferred backbone
initialization, seed, batch order, 6,000 updates, AdamW/cosine schedule,
loss, and validation-best checkpoint rule.  The existing BSR selected step
5,300; the Oracle model selected step 6,000.  On 4,096 matched test examples,
BSR scores 9.631 and Oracle-Residual-BSR 16.767 dB target SI-SDRi.
The paired improvement is $+7.136$ dB (recording-day 95\% interval
$[6.970,7.282]$), with all three held-out days positive.  The combined
high-resolution prior and explicit innovation interface therefore benefits
this controlled separator.  Actual $N_t$ is unavailable at deployment;
this two-model comparison also does not isolate adapter capacity from the
condition information.

\paragraph{Causal high-resolution residual conditions.}
We then replace the current-background Oracle map with two conditions computed
strictly from the preceding 60 s.  Each of twelve 5-s history windows has the
same $79\times128$ log-magnitude grid.  Annotation-overlapping windows are
masked; when one is unavailable, its slot copies the nearest annotation-clean
window still inside the past 60 s.  This affects 235 of 68,075 eligible
current windows.  Persistence uses the last clean history map.  A lightweight
GRU encodes each map into four temporal chunks, processes twelve steps, and
decodes a residual correction to persistence.  It predicts the entire future
$79\times128$ log-background map, without seeing the current waveform.
The predictor is trained for 3,000 updates on training-day clean backgrounds
and frozen at its lowest validation log-magnitude MAE.  Validation MAE is
0.5456 for the GRU versus 0.5518 for persistence (1.12\% relative gain).
On the held-out test days, the corresponding map MAEs are 0.5308 and 0.5386.

For every condition map $B$, we form $R=\log|X_t|-B$ and feed
$[X_t,B,R]$ through precisely the Oracle residual adapter.  The Zero
control repeats the fixed training-set mean log background at every frame.
The Shuffled control draws an annotation-clean $B_j$ from a \emph{different
recording day}: training and testing draw within their respective splits;
validation draws from training days because validation has only one day.
Persistence takes the last clean history map; Forecast uses the frozen GRU.
All four separators start from the same transferred BSR backbone and zero
adapter, so their initial waveform output is identical to ordinary BSR.
They use identical synthetic events, training-example order, 6,000 update
budget, optimizer, loss, validation-best checkpoint rule, and held-out
mixtures.  Thus the four residual arms differ only in the source of $B$.

Table~\ref{tab:causalresidual} separates interface and background effects.
Zero exceeds ordinary BSR by $+2.525$ dB (recording-day paired 95\% CI
$[2.262,2.701]$); Shuffled exceeds BSR by $+2.313$ dB
($[2.027,2.905]$). Hence the full BSR-to-causal gain cannot be attributed

and the current mixture log-spectrum supplied by the innovation path, so it
does not separately identify parameter count. With the adapter held fixed,
Persistence exceeds Zero by $+2.138$ dB ($[1.775,2.377]$) and wrong-day

beyond architecture and arbitrary background maps. Forecast exceeds
Persistence by only $+0.056$ dB ($[0.036,0.074]$), again positive on all
three days but small in practice. Forecast retains 66.1\% of the Oracle
gain over BSR, with a $2.416$ dB Oracle gap ($[2.114,2.640]$).

22.871 dB ($\Delta=-0.599$, CI $[-0.851,-0.346]$); even the oracle is lower.
This exposes conditioning/capacity effects that the simpler diagnostic misses.
Figure~\ref{fig:strength} gives the more informative strength sweep.  Forecast
conditioning helps weak targets by 0.728, 0.643, 0.525, 0.366, and 0.157 dB at
$-18,-15,-12,-9,-6$ dB, respectively, then loses from $-3$ dB onward.  The
shuffled condition also helps the weakest targets but collapses as SNR rises;
therefore weak-target gain alone cannot prove that temporal forecast accuracy,
rather than regularization or extra capacity, is responsible.

\subsection{Real events without separated ground truth}
Following learned reference-free metrics \cite{kilgour2019fad,grais2019referenceless}
and semantic audio embeddings \cite{wu2023clap,xiao2024clapscore}, we train,
calibrate, and freeze a binary event classifier (test AUROC .9723, AP .9755,
F1 .9275).  Mixture consistency imposes $\hat N=x-\hat s$.  We restore
$\hat s$ at gains $\{-60,-30,-24,-18,-12,-6,0\}$ dB and test whether event
probability recovers monotonically.  Identity and all-to-event controls reject
trivial solutions; changes outside annotated intervals audit preservation.

We evaluate the four frozen 128-group residual models on the exact 103 test
calls used by the preliminary FiLM audit. Each call receives twelve strictly
earlier annotation-clean maps from the same WAV. Six histories each lack one
clean window, filled by the nearest clean window still inside the prior 60 s.
Zero uses the training mean, Shuffled a clean background from another test
day, Persistence the last historical map, and Forecast the frozen GRU applied
to that history. No condition reads the current call. Table~\ref{tab:events}
and Fig.~\ref{fig:real} show the results.

Persistence versus Zero lowers $p(\hat N)$ by $.0624$ (recording-day paired
95\% CI $[-.0682,-.0547]$), raises $p(\hat s)$ by $.0469$
($[.0198,.1006]$), and lowers off-event change by $.0255$
($[-.0386,-.0077]$). Forecast shows the same directions versus Zero, with
$\Delta p(\hat N)=-.0531$ and $\Delta$ off-event $=-.0260$.
However, wrong-day Shuffled attains lower $p(\hat N)$ and higher $p(\hat s)$
than either causal condition: Persistence minus Shuffled is $+.0471$
($[.0317,.0706]$) for $p(\hat N)$ and $-.0606$ ($[-.0731,-.0404]$) for
$p(\hat s)$. Persistence protects off-event audio much better
($\Delta=-.0408$, $[-.0519,-.0320]$). Monotonicity is near saturation for
all four models and does not separate them. The classifier probabilities
therefore do not reproduce the controlled full-reference ranking; they can
reward aggressive extraction, while off-event preservation favors the causal
conditions. Isolated real-event stems remain unavailable.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The main-domain 30-band Oracle is a diagnostic of the existing interface: even when FiLM receives
$E(N_t)$ from the exact current clean waveform, its held-out mean does not
exceed the mixture-only BS-RoFormer, and its paired interval spans zero.
The full-waveform copy check succeeds exactly, so the current negative result
cannot be interpreted as evidence that the true background lacks useful
information.  It leaves the relative roles of 30-band compression and FiLM
injection unresolved.  The fixed-head ladder shows a clear representation
bottleneck: even mixture-phase conditions retain a confirmed gain at 128
equal-width frequency groups or 1-s full-bin temporal pooling, whereas 64
equal-width groups and 30 geometric bands do not.  The original FiLM result
cannot be assigned to this bottleneck alone because its interface differs.
The trained 128-group Oracle-Residual-BSR gains $+7.136$ dB on matched
held-out mixtures, showing that a high-resolution true background and
explicit innovation can help the BSR backbone.  Replacing true current
background with past-60-s persistence or a frozen GRU forecast retains large
gains of $+4.664$ and $+4.720$ dB over BSR. The fixed-mean Zero and
wrong-day Shuffled controls also gain $+2.525$ and $+2.313$ dB, respectively;
therefore part of the BSR-to-causal gain comes from the expanded interface.
Holding that interface fixed, Persistence gains $+2.138$ dB over Zero and
$+2.351$ dB over Shuffled. Correctly timed history therefore provides useful
information beyond a generic background map. The forecast adds a consistent
but modest $+0.056$ dB over persistence on three test days; the remaining
$2.416$ dB Oracle gap shows headroom for a better causal prior. The Zero
control cannot distinguish parameter capacity from access to the mixture log
spectrum in the innovation path. Synthetic-target supervision and only three
held-out days also limit generalization.
Zero-FiLM is effectively tied with BS-RoFormer, while Forecast is no better
than either Zero or Shuffled.  The result does not isolate prediction error
as the bottleneck; a five-second, 30-band static summary and its FiLM interface
may be too coarse for the separator's time-frequency tokens.  FiLM remains the
best interface on controlled spectral surrogates, and the secondary unlabeled
WAV sweep shows weak-target gains, but neither establishes a main-domain gain.
The new real-call audit has a mixed outcome: causal maps improve all three
semantic/preservation measures against Zero, but Shuffled wins the two
classifier event probabilities while losing markedly on off-event change.
Forecast is not uniformly better than Persistence. Thus the real calls do
not establish that correctly timed history improves semantic separation;
they expose a preservation-versus-detection tradeoff under a surrogate
classifier. The old 30-band FiLM real-call comparison remains preliminary.
Only three held-out days and no isolated real stems limit this inference.

The existing 30-band FiLM frontend is usable for inspection: on a CPU smoke test, one 5-s
chunk ran at RTF 0.014 after history features were prepared; the interface
displays forecasts and permits playback and download of both estimates.  This
does not establish deployment of the new 128-group residual model or field use.
Further work should improve prediction of event-relevant time-frequency
structure rather than only mean log-magnitude MAE, cover more sites and
seasons, and obtain isolated
or stronger partial references for real events.  Classifier-based restoration
is evidence of semantic transfer, not a replacement for waveform fidelity.
