\begin{abstract}
We test whether a background spectrum forecast from strictly preceding ocean
audio helps separate the next 5-s acoustic event.  FC-BSR injects the forecast
into BS-RoFormer by concatenation, feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM), or
cross-attention.  A controlled 13-family study selects FiLM: at fixed 0-dB
input SNR it improves SI-SDR improvement (SI-SDRi) by 0.692 dB (95\% paired
bootstrap interval $[0.542,0.847]$).  On 42 h of continuous hydrophone
background, however, forecast conditioning is strength dependent: it exceeds
the unconditioned model by 0.728 dB at $-18$ dB and 0.525 dB at $-12$ dB, then
falls behind above approximately $-4$ dB.  On 1,276 annotated real whale calls
without isolated stems, FC-BSR slightly raises event-stem probability but does
not reduce residual event probability or off-event change.  Thus the forecast
prior is useful in controlled and weak-target regimes, while its benefit on
real unknown events remains unproven.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
ocean acoustics, source separation, soundscape forecasting, BS-RoFormer,
weak-reference evaluation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Ocean backgrounds vary with shipping, weather, biology, and season
\cite{miksisolds2013indian}.  A mixture-only separator must infer both an
unexpected event and this changing background from the same observation.  We
instead ask whether the preceding soundscape can supply a causal prior:
\begin{equation}
 S_{t-H:t-1}\xrightarrow{P_\psi}\widehat S_t,\qquad
 x_t=N_t+\alpha s_t\xrightarrow{F_\phi(\cdot\mid\widehat S_t)}(\hat s_t,\hat N_t),
\end{equation}
where the separator never observes the current clean background $N_t$.

Time-domain separators \cite{luo2019convtasnet,luo2020dprnn}, attention models
\cite{subakan2021sepformer,wang2023tfgridnet}, and band-split systems
\cite{luo2023bsrnn,lu2023bsroformer} provide strong mixture-only backbones.
Hybrid Transformers and MixIT broaden the architecture and supervision regimes
\cite{rouard2023htdemucs,wisdom2020mixit}, but do not test a forecast of the
upcoming background.  FiLM, cross-attention, and cross-modal separation
\cite{perez2018film,vaswani2017attention,zhao2018pixels} motivate three
interfaces.  Forecasting baselines range from recurrent to linear and
patch-based models \cite{salinas2020deepar,oreshkin2020nbeats,zeng2023dlinear,nie2023patchtst}.

Our contributions are: (1) FC-BSR,\footnote{Code and reproduction instructions:
\url{https://github.com/D-eval/ocean_noise_pred_splite}.} with matched
concatenation, FiLM, and cross-attention ablations; (2) a 13-family marine-aware
source generator containing whale-like sweeps and vessel processes; (3) a
5-s real-background strength sweep with baseline, oracle, forecast, and
shuffled conditions; and (4) a matched BS-RoFormer comparison on untouched
annotated whale calls.  The evidence is deliberately tiered so that synthetic
ground truth is not conflated with semantic transfer.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Forecast and conditional separator}
Historical one-third-octave levels (TOL)
$S_{t-H:t-1}\in\mathbb R^{H\times F}$ are normalized using training data only.
A residual GRU predicts
\begin{equation}
 z_t=\operatorname{LN}(\operatorname{GRU}(S_{t-H:t-1})),\quad
 \widehat S_t=S_{t-1}+g_\psi(z_t).
\end{equation}
Residual initialization represents persistence exactly; a learned predictor is
retained only if validation MAE improves upon persistence
\cite{gneiting2011pointForecast}.

BS-RoFormer maps the mixture STFT to band tokens $X_k$.  At the same location
before its axial Transformer, we compare
\begin{align}
\text{Concat: }\widetilde X_k&=X_k+f([X_k;P_k(\widehat S_t)]),\nonumber\\
\text{FiLM: }\widetilde X_k&=(1+\tanh\gamma_k)\odot X_k+\beta_k,\label{eq:inject}\\
\text{X-attn: }\widetilde X&=X+\operatorname{Attn}(X,E(\widehat S_t),E(\widehat S_t)).\nonumber
\end{align}
All residual branches begin at the identity.  The loss combines waveform
$L_1$ and multi-resolution complex-STFT losses; the TOL-surrogate study adds
$0.2\|\widehat S_t-S_t\|_1$.  Predictors are frozen before field-waveform
separator training.

\subsection{Marine-aware source distribution}
The generator contains 13 families: harmonic, linear chirp, inharmonic,
impulse train, frequency-modulated tone, band noise, whale upsweep, downsweep,
arch, S-curve, pulse packet, vessel tonal, and vessel cavitation.  Whale proxies
use one to three localized events with variable duration, curvature, partials,
taper, and flutter.  Vessel-tonal and narrow-band cavitation components draw a
5--20 Hz low-frequency-oscillator amplitude envelope with probability 0.8;
their broadband component is not modulated.  Training SNR is uniform on
$[-12,12]$ dB.  This distribution is intended to cover contour and modulation
patterns, not to claim species identity.

We quantify spectral displacement with directional $D_{\rm KL}(p\|q)$ and
finite symmetric Jensen--Shannon (JS) divergence \cite{lin1991divergence};
spectral entropy and source--ocean overlap are reported separately.  Larger
KL/JS expands coverage but is not treated as semantic diversity.

\subsection{Evidence ladder and protocol}
Tier 1 uses NOAA--Navy SanctSound hourly TOL products
\cite{noaa2020sanctsound}.  MB03 deployments 01--02/03/04 are
train/validation/test.  Random-phase inversion creates controlled spectral
surrogates, not recovered audio.  Every waveform is 5 s; 4,000 examples per
training deployment and 2,500 updates are used.

Tier 2 uses 42 h of continuous 8-kHz hydrophone WAV (12/6/24 h for
train/validation/test).  The exact clean current waveform $N_t$ is mixed with
a synthetic target.  The principal experiment uses 5-s mixtures and 2,500
updates, and evaluates 11 SNRs from $-18$ to $+12$ dB.  A separate
predictor-resolution diagnostic holds history duration at one hour and changes
its step to 5 s, 30 s, 1 min, or 10 min; all later experiments return to 5-s
waveforms.

Tier 3 uses day-disjoint 2-kHz DCLDE 2013 right-whale recordings
\cite{gillespie2019dclde}.  Event annotations support detection
\cite{shiu2020marine}, but isolated stems do not exist.  Annotation-clean
training clips receive the marine-aware proxies; 1,276 untouched real positive
clips are evaluated.  Both arms share samples, seeds, core initialization,
2,500-update budget, and validation-only checkpoint selection.

Complete-reference tiers report SI-SDR and SI-SDRi using scale-invariant
projection \cite{vincent2006sdr,leroux2019sdr}.  All uncertainty intervals are
seeded paired bootstraps; continuous clips are block-bootstrapped by recording
hour and real events by held-out day.  Current test spectra are supervision or
oracle controls only; causal histories end before $t$.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\subsection{Conditioning selection and source diversity}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} selects FiLM solely by validation performance.  On
832 fixed-0-dB pairs (64 per family), it improves 4.850 to 5.541 dB SI-SDRi.
Band-wise scale and bias can directly encode ``expected energy here''; concat
must compress doubled features, while cross-attention must learn alignment from
a coarse condition under the same budget.

FiLM gains 0.869, 0.835, 0.919, 0.906, and 0.806 dB on the five whale proxy

divergence broadens support but does not predict usefulness.

The harder fixed-5-s experiment compares BS-RoFormer, current-spectrum oracle,
causal forecast, and time-shuffled condition on 1,152 paired test mixtures.
Averaged across training SNRs, the baseline scores 23.470 dB and Forecast-FC-BSR
22.871 dB ($\Delta=-0.599$, CI $[-0.851,-0.346]$); even the oracle is lower.
This exposes conditioning/capacity effects that the simpler diagnostic misses.
Figure~\ref{fig:strength} gives the more informative strength sweep.  Forecast
conditioning helps weak targets by 0.728, 0.643, 0.525, 0.366, and 0.157 dB at
$-18,-15,-12,-9,-6$ dB, respectively, then loses from $-3$ dB onward.  The
shuffled condition also helps the weakest targets but collapses as SNR rises;
therefore weak-target gain alone cannot prove that temporal forecast accuracy,
rather than regularization or extra capacity, is responsible.

\subsection{Real events without separated ground truth}
Following learned reference-free metrics \cite{kilgour2019fad,grais2019referenceless}
and semantic audio embeddings \cite{wu2023clap,xiao2024clapscore}, we train,
calibrate, and freeze a binary event classifier (test AUROC .9723, AP .9755,
F1 .9275).  Mixture consistency imposes $\hat N=x-\hat s$.  We restore
$\hat s$ at gains $\{-60,-30,-24,-18,-12,-6,0\}$ dB and test whether event
probability recovers monotonically.  Identity and all-to-event controls reject
trivial solutions; changes outside annotated intervals audit preservation.

The 12-by-5-s forecaster reduces validation MAE from 2.076 to 1.974 dB and is
then frozen.  On proxy validation, BS-RoFormer and FC-BSR score 11.125 and
11.088 dB SI-SDRi.  Table~\ref{tab:events} shows the untouched-call result.
FC-BSR produces a slightly more event-like event stem ($+.0051$), but residual
event probability is higher, monotonicity is unchanged, and off-event change
increases.  The day-block interval for residual probability difference
$+.0145$ is $[-.0177,.0269]$.  Hence this tier demonstrates separator transfer
to real events, not an advantage from forecast conditioning.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The evidence supports a narrower claim than the initial hypothesis.  FiLM is
the best conditioning interface on controlled stems, and the prior helps weak
targets in 5-s real backgrounds.  It is not universally beneficial: the gain
reverses for stronger targets, an oracle condition does not rescue the average,
and no forecast advantage appears on untouched whale calls.  Persistence also
wins three of four predictor resolutions.  These negative controls are central:
they separate the existence of useful background information from a claim that
the present architecture always exploits it.

The system is practical for inspection: all field-waveform variants run at
RTF about .0028, and a local frontend displays forecasts and permits playback
and download of both estimates.  This does not establish field deployment.
Future work should condition only under calibrated uncertainty, test multiple
sites and seasons, and obtain isolated or stronger partial references for real
events.  Classifier-based restoration is evidence of semantic transfer, not a
replacement for waveform fidelity.
